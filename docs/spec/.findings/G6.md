## CONTRADICTIONS

- Rotate every secret versus preserve the APP_KEY - 22.7 requires "Every secret has been rotated" and 22.11 step 37 says "Rotate every credential: panel API keys, SFTP passwords, database passwords, and any token that touched the old box", and 22.6 step 2 says the environment file is "recreated with **freshly rotated secrets** - never reuse the old ones" VS 22.12 step 7 says restore `.env` with the "**identical `APP_KEY`**... **Do not regenerate the key.** If a tool offers to, refuse" and 22-3 requires "The `APP_KEY` was preserved unchanged" - the APP_KEY is a secret living in the same `.env` file that 22.6 says to recreate with fresh secrets, so a literal reading of 22.7/22.6 destroys encrypted Panel data permanently - the builder cannot decide whether "every secret" carves out the APP_KEY, and the mistake is irreversible.
- Four worlds versus five worlds - Section 20 Phase 2 says "**All five worlds created**, borders set, pregenerated with Chunky" VS 22.9 lists the irreplaceable world folders as "(overworld, nether, end, resource world)" - four - and 22.11 step 21 requires "correct borders on all **four** worlds" - a migration verified against four worlds will silently leave the fifth world unchecked or uncopied - the builder cannot tell which the fifth world is or whether it is exempt from migration verification.
- Wings port 8080 closed but the Panel is on another box - 22.11 step 3 says "Port 8080 stays closed to the internet; the Panel reaches Wings over it internally" VS 22.10 says "Install Wings on the new VPS, register it as a second node on the **Panel you already have**", meaning the Panel is on the old VPS and the connection is not internal at all - step 6 then requires a "**green heartbeat**" and says "If it does not, stop here" - as written the firewall list guarantees the heartbeat fails, and the builder cannot decide whether to open 8080 to the world, restrict it to the Panel IP, or tunnel it.
- Container-allocation formula disagrees with its own acceptance criterion - 22.13 states the allocation should be "Heap plus at least 25 per cent, or 768 MB minimum" VS 22-8 requires "`-Xmx` is at least 25 per cent **below** the container allocation" - these are different arithmetic: a 9 GB heap plus 25 per cent gives an 11.25 GB allocation, but 9 GB is only 20 per cent below 11.25 GB, so a build that follows 22.13's formula fails 22-8 - the builder cannot decide which side of the ratio the 25 per cent applies to.
- Sizing guide leaves no RAM outside the heap - 22.3 gives "40 to 60 players, 4 dedicated, **8 GB**, Heap **~6 GB**" and "Up to 24, 2 dedicated, **4 GB**, Heap **~2.5 GB**" VS 22.13's own split reserves "Panel, MariaDB, Redis 4 GB" plus "**Unallocated, left for the OS** 12 GB or more" and requires heap plus 25 per cent as the container allocation - 6 GB heap needs a 7.5 GB allocation on an 8 GB box, leaving 0.5 GB for the OS, Wings, MariaDB and Redis - the builder cannot tell whether the 22.3 RAM column means whole-box RAM or the game container allocation.
- Phase 8 gate requires tests that only Phase 9 can produce - Section 20 Phase 8 gate says "**every acceptance test in Section 21 passes**" and Section 21's header says every row must pass "**before the first paying player connects**" VS row 51 requires "**One full week of normal play** produces zero punishments of honest players" and Phase 9 is where the "small group of paying players" first appears, and Phase 9's own gate is "one complete season" - row 51's evidence, and arguably rows 34 and 36 in their real form, cannot exist before paying players connect - the builder cannot decide whether soft-launch players count as "the first paying player" for the purposes of the launch gate.
- Never auto-punish versus the auto-mute test - Section 25 rejects "**Automated moderation that punishes without review** - False positives cost paying customers. **Always alert, never auto-punish** (14.1)" and 14b requires "code review confirms no punishment path exists from detector output" VS row 48 requires "200 messages per second results in an **auto-mute**" - a mute is a punishment applied without review - the builder cannot decide whether chat flooding is a carve-out from the never-auto-punish rule or whether row 48 should be an alert plus rate-limit instead.
- Bounty system versus the wagering detector - 26.2 Tier 1 item 4 proposes "**A bounty system.** Players place Berry bounties on other players" and calls it "close to a perfect feature" VS 14a defines the abuse signature as exactly "two accounts fight, one dies, the loser pays the winner within 60 seconds. The **combat-correlated signature fires**" - a bounty payout is a Berry transfer correlated with a combat death and will fire the detector on every legitimate bounty, while 14b demands staged innocent payments produce zero sanctions - the builder cannot ship bounties without an owner ruling on how bounty payouts are exempted from the detector.
- Under-30-minute rebuild versus the stated timings - 22.5 step 2 says "Deploy the full stack from the repository. **If this takes more than 30 minutes, the portability contract is broken - fix the repository, not the host**" and row 3 requires "Full stack rebuilt on a brand-new machine in **under 30 minutes**" VS 22.14 budgets "VPS provisioning, hardening, firewall 20 to 30 min / 45 to 60 min", "Docker and Wings install 15 min / 30 to 45 min" and "Node creation and green heartbeat 10 min / 20 to 40 min" - even the rehearsed column sums to 45 minutes before a world exists - the builder cannot tell whether the 30-minute clock excludes provisioning, hardening and Wings, and therefore cannot tell when to declare the portability contract broken.
- Two servers never together - the never-break rule (and Section 30.2, cited) forbids running both Paper servers at once VS 22.13 says of `laughtail-dev` "Now it can run **at the same time** as production. This is the real luxury the bigger box buys" and "On the new box, run both, and **delete that constraint from the runbook**" - the rule's death is conditional on hardware the project does not have yet - the builder cannot decide the exact threshold at which the constraint is removed, and removing it early freezes production rather than crashing it.
- Fix-do-not-add versus Tier 1 in the first month - Section 20 Phase 9 says "**Fix, do not add.** Run one full season, including one real reset and one real Champion, before opening more widely" VS 26.2 is titled "Tier 1 - the retention engine, **first month after launch**" and lists clans, quests, a battle pass and bounties - the first month after launch is the soft-launch season - the builder cannot decide whether Tier 1 work starts during the soft-launch season or only after it closes.
- 22.6 is superseded but its acceptance criteria are not - 22.6 is marked "**SUPERSEDED BY SECTION 22.11**... Where this subsection and 22.11 disagree, 22.11 wins" VS 22.7 immediately follows it saying "These are the general criteria... **both sets must pass** before the old server is deleted", and 22.7's criteria are written against 22.6's manual full-stack procedure (for example "Every secret has been rotated", which 22.11 step 37 narrows to credentials) - the builder cannot tell which of 22.7's eight boxes still apply to a node transfer.
- Player cap asserted versus player cap measured - Section 20 Phase 6 says "**The player cap set from the measured number**" and row 21 requires the cap "set from the measured result" VS 22.3 states "Up to 24 ... Current box. **Adequate for launch**" - one says the number is an output of measurement, the other pre-declares it - the builder cannot tell whether 24 is a target to validate or a ceiling to obey if measurement says 18.
- Cross-server networking rejected at launch versus arena splitting at 60 players - Section 25 rejects "**Cross-server networking at launch** - Correct at scale (12.4), wrong on 2 cores. It is in Tier 3" and 26.4 places "Velocity proxy with the arena on its own instance" in Tier 3 "after migration" VS 22.3 says at "60 to 100" players "Consider splitting the arena to a second instance behind a proxy" and 22.13 lists an "Arena or event instance, 8 GB allocation, 6 GB heap" as merely "Optional" - a builder sizing the new box cannot tell whether the arena instance is a Tier 3 project requiring its own decision or a normal part of the migration.

## AMBIGUITIES

- MSPT measurement method - rows 19 and 20 require "Under 25 ms" and "Under 40 ms", and Phase 6's gate repeats 25 ms - no statistic (mean, median, p95, p99), no sample duration, and no definition of "normal play" is given - Is the 25 ms figure a mean or a 95th percentile, and over how many minutes of sampling?
- Row 14 grep term "key" is unusable as written - row 14 requires a repo grep for "betting, gambling, crate, **key**, lottery, spin, casino, wager, stake, coinflip and dice" to find nothing - the repo necessarily contains APP_KEY, API key, SSH key and licence-key strings, and 22.9 and 22.12 mandate APP_KEY handling text - no exclusion list, case rule, or word-boundary rule is given - Should row 14 match whole words in game-logic paths only, and what exclusion list makes it pass?
- What "the migration script" in row 78 is - row 78 requires "The migration script has been executed successfully at least once, end to end" with evidence "Migration log" - Section 22 contains a runbook of manual steps, two `mysqldump` invocations, `scripts/healthcheck.sh` and `scripts/drift.sh`, but names no migration script; Phase 0 separately mentions "Database with schema migrations" - Does row 78 mean the database schema migration tool, or a script that automates 22.11?
- The migration tool is named only once - 22.11 step 15 says "confirm the schema version matches what **Flyway** expects" while 22-2 says only "what the migration tool expects" and Phase 0 says "Database with schema migrations" - no section in my set selects the tool - Is Flyway the chosen schema migration tool, or is step 15 an example?
- Steal-time threshold - 22-10 requires steal time "effectively zero under load" and 22.11 step 8 says "A **non-zero steady** steal percentage means the host is overselling and you should stop the migration" - no numeric threshold, no sample window beyond `mpstat 1 10` - What steal percentage over what window aborts the migration?
- Rollback leaves two databases - 22.11 restores the game database onto the new box at steps 13-15, then transfers the server at step 16, and step 17 says on failure "the server record stays on the old node. **Start it there and you are back in production**" - nothing states which database the rolled-back server connects to, whether the new database must be discarded, or how divergence is prevented if players then play on the old node - After a failed transfer, is the old database authoritative, and what must be done to the restored copy?
- mysqldump command is under-specified - 22.11 step 13 gives `mysqldump --single-transaction --routines --triggers -u root -p laughtail > ...` - no `--databases` (so the dump contains no CREATE DATABASE and the target schema must pre-exist), no character set flag, no events flag, and no statement of the target database name on the new box - Should the restore target schema be created in advance with a specified character set and collation, and what is it named?
- Maintenance mode is referenced but never defined - 22.6 step 5 and 22.11 step 11 both say "enable maintenance mode", and step 34 says "Disable maintenance mode and announce" - no mechanism, plugin, flag, or player-facing message is specified - What implements maintenance mode: whitelist off, a proxy MOTD, or a plugin flag?
- Announced window length versus downtime - 22.6 and 22.11 both say "Publish the window at least 48 hours in advance", 22.14 says "**Player-facing downtime: 30 to 90 minutes**" and also "Announce a two-hour window and finish early", while 22.7 requires "The whole procedure completes inside the announced window" and total wall clock is "3 to 4 hours rehearsed, a full day the first time" - it is unclear whether the announced window covers downtime only or the whole procedure - Does the announced window measure player-facing downtime or total wall clock?
- 22.14 first-time totals do not reconcile - summing the first-time column gives roughly 4 to 6 hours, yet the text says "a full day the first time" and "Budget the full day" - no explanation of the gap - Is the full day a contingency buffer or does it include work not listed in the table?
- Heap values are approximate - 22.3 gives heaps as "~2.5 GB", "~6 GB", "~10 GB", "~12 GB" while 22-8 is a hard pass/fail on the heap-to-allocation ratio - a tilde value cannot be tested - What exact `-Xmx` value is required at each sizing tier?
- Row 24 command latency - "Every command responds within 100 ms, or acknowledges and runs async" - no percentile, no definition of "responds" (packet sent versus GUI painted), no list of commands under test - Is 100 ms a hard maximum for every invocation or a 95th percentile across a named command list?
- Row 51 week of normal play - "One full week of normal play produces zero punishments of honest players" - no minimum player count, no minimum hours played, and no definition of who certifies a player honest - What minimum concurrent-player-hours constitutes the qualifying week?
- Watchdog degradation ladder not enumerated here - Phase 6 requires "the watchdog built and its **degradation ladder** verified" and row 22 requires "each degradation step **in order**" with recovery - my sections never list the steps or their trigger thresholds, and Section 25 simultaneously rejects "Anti-lag plugins... They fix a symptom by deleting player property" - What are the ordered degradation steps, and does any step remove player property?
- Phase 9 group size - "A small group of paying players" - no number, no selection method, no expansion trigger beyond "one complete season" - How many players are in the soft-launch group and what metric authorises widening?
- Row 27 minimum spread - "Buy exceeds sell by the minimum spread at both extremes of the dynamic band" - the numeric spread and the band limits are not stated in my sections - What is the minimum spread percentage and the dynamic band's floor and ceiling?
- Rows 13, 38 and 15 rely on a "written audit" or "screenshots" with no template - no format, no reviewer, no storage location beyond docs/acceptance.md - What must a written audit contain to count as evidence, and who signs it?
- Pelican CPU limit for the current box - 22.11 step 18 explains "`200` means two cores, `800` means eight" and 22.13 recommends "`400` to `600` on an 8-core box" leaving headroom - no recommended value for the present 2-core box - What CPU limit should the 2 vCPU box use given Wings, Panel and backups also need CPU?
- Firewall list omits Panel-facing ports - 22.11 step 3 lists 22/tcp, 2022/tcp, 25565/tcp, 24454/udp, 19132/udp and `ufw enable`, with 8080 closed - no rule for 80/443 (needed if the Panel moves per 22.12) and no source-restriction syntax - Which ports and source IPs does a Wings-only node actually need?
- "If in scope" appears twice for Bedrock - 22.11 step 3 says "19132/udp # Bedrock via Geyser, **if in scope**" and step 27 says "Bedrock connects, **if in scope**", while 22.7 states flatly "Bedrock connectivity works on the new host" and rows 62, 37 and 65 assume Bedrock exists - Is Bedrock in scope for launch or not?
- Section 26 tiers have no gates - 26.1 to 26.4 order the work but define no acceptance criteria, no owner of the decision to move between tiers, and no rule for whether a Tier 1 feature must pass Section 21-style tests - Do post-launch tiers require their own acceptance rows before shipping to paying players?

## RISKS ON A 2 vCPU / 4 GB HOST

- Phase 6 requires a "bot load test at 10, 20, 30 and 40" while 22.3 rates the current box at "Up to 24" concurrent players - the 30 and 40 runs are designed to exceed the box, so MSPT will collapse; if the bot clients are also run on the game box the measurement is worthless and the box may swap.
- 22.3's own current-box row, "4 GB, Heap ~2.5 GB", leaves roughly 1.5 GB for the OS, Docker, Wings, the Panel, MariaDB and Redis, against 22.13's split which budgets 4 GB for "Panel, MariaDB, Redis" alone - memory pressure here shows up as swap and long GC pauses, not a clean crash.
- 22.13 argues that unallocated RAM matters because "The OS page cache holds hot chunk data, and this genuinely improves chunk load times" - on 4 GB with a 2.5 GB heap there is almost no page cache, so chunk loads hit disk and MSPT spikes when players spread out.
- The Pelican transfer "archives the volume on the old node, streams it to the new one" - tar plus gzip of the world directory is CPU-bound and will saturate one of two cores; on a 2 vCPU box this must only happen with the server stopped, and 22.14 already budgets "10 to 40 min" for it.
- `mysqldump --single-transaction --routines --triggers` on the game database competes with MariaDB's own buffer pool and the Panel on the same 4 GB - run it only with the game server stopped, as 22.11 step 12 requires.
- 22.11 step 10 requires restoring the backup "onto the new box into a throwaway schema" and 22-12 requires a full restore drill; Phase 0 requires a restore drill into "a scratch environment" - a scratch stack alongside the live stack does not fit in 4 GB and also collides with the never-run-both-servers rule.
- Row 20 permits 40 ms MSPT "at the event cap with combat active" while Phase 7 requires War events "with full state snapshots" - full inventory and arena snapshots are large synchronous serialisations; on two cores this is the most likely single source of a multi-second freeze.
- Row 60 requires "Maximum cosmetics on all players adds under 2 ms MSPT" and Phase 7 sets a particle budget - particle spawning scales with player count times effect count and is main-thread work on the same two cores that run combat.
- Row 48 requires surviving "200 messages per second... with no MSPT impact" - chat handling plus the flood detector plus logging at that rate is a real CPU burst on a 2 vCPU box.
- Row 49's packet-abuse suite is deliberately CPU-hostile; with only two cores there is no spare core to absorb it, so a pass here is far harder than on the 4-core target in 22.3.
- Phase 2's Chunky pregeneration of five worlds is sustained single-core chunk generation plus heavy disk write on shared NVMe - it must not overlap any measurement or any other server.
- Phase 3 puts the arbitrage audit "in CI and nightly" - a nightly full-graph audit over all items and recipe chains on the game box competes with backups and the Panel's cron; both are scheduled work on the same two cores.
- Phase 8 puts the live map, website, leaderboards and Discord bot in scope while 22.13 concedes "Section 5 wanted these off the game box; now they can come home" - on the current box a map renderer or bot on the game host is a direct MSPT risk.
- Voice on 24454/udp plus Geyser on 19132/udp plus Java on 25565 means three network stacks and Geyser's protocol translation on two cores; Geyser translation cost scales with Bedrock player count.
- Section 26 Tier 1 and Tier 2 (clans, daily and weekly quests, a battle pass, bounties, King of the Hill, supply drops) are scheduled for "first month after launch" and "months two and three", which is before the migration in Section 22 - each adds tick work, scheduled jobs and database writes to the 2 vCPU box that was already rated for only 24 players.
- 22.13's "Pelican CPU limit... `100` is one core" combined with no stated value for a 2-core box invites setting 200 (both cores) and starving Wings, the Panel, MariaDB and backups, which manifests as backup failures and heartbeat loss rather than as game lag.
