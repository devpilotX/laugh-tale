## CONTRADICTIONS

- Staff exemption from equality - Law 2 section 2 states equality with "No exceptions, not for donors, not for friends, not for staff" VS 3.2 which states "Admin and staff tooling is the only permitted exception" and 3.6's parenthetical "except genuine admin tools" - it matters because acceptance criterion 3-6/3-3 says "No permission node, config key, or database column grants any paying player a capability another paying player lacks", and staff who also paid for access would fail that grep-and-review test by definition - a builder cannot decide whether staff accounts are excluded from the equality audit, or must hold their staff powers on separate non-paying accounts.
- Crates banned here or banned elsewhere - 3.5's classification table says real-money crates, keys, mystery boxes and spin wheels are "Banned under ... The equality rules in 3.2 and 3.6, **not** this section" VS 3.5's own "Forbidden outright" list which begins "Paid crates, paid keys, paid mystery boxes, paid spin wheels, paid lucky blocks" - it matters because 3.5 explicitly instructs "Keep that separation intact" so a future contributor relaxing one rule does not relax the other, yet the same subsection both disclaims and asserts the crate ban - a builder cannot decide which subsection the crate prohibition is recorded under when writing docs/decisions.md and docs/rejected.md entries.
- `/crate` absolutely banned versus conditionally allowed - 3.5.1 Layer 1 states the following "must not exist, must not be registered by any plugin", listing `/crate` among them VS 3.9's grep criterion which passes a hit on `crate` if it is "a deterministic reward with published contents", and 3.5 permits "Earned reward chests where the player *chooses* the reward from a visible set" - it matters because the earned-cosmetics system in Section 11 plausibly wants a crate-like command - a builder cannot decide whether a free, earned, published-contents chest may be bound to `/crate` or must use a different command name.
- Random rewards with published odds - 3.5 forbids "Any random reward whose odds are hidden", which by omission permits random rewards with published odds VS 3.6's sell table row "Crates, keys, random anything | Never (3.5)" and 3.5.1's instruction to build "No stake-holding, no coin flip, no dice, no duel wager, no lottery" - it matters for any earned reward pool design - a builder cannot decide whether server-side randomness with a published odds table is permitted at all, or only non-random deterministic choice.
- False ban refunds - 3.5.1 justifies alert-only detection on the grounds that "on this server a false ban is a refund plus a public accusation of unfairness" VS 3.8 which requires the refund policy to "State clearly: bans are not refunded. Say it before purchase, not after." - it matters because the two statements imply opposite published policy text and opposite handling of a wrongful ban - a builder cannot write the Refund Policy page (acceptance item 3-4) without the owner stating whether an overturned ban is refunded, re-whitelisted for free, or neither.
- Fail closed versus never auto-punish - Law 8 states "If a check cannot run, deny the action" VS 3.5.1 which states `/pay` "is a legitimate, necessary command that cannot be removed", "Flag for staff review. Never auto-punish. This is a hard rule, not a preference", and acceptance item 3-8 requiring "no code path to an automated sanction" - it matters for the failure behaviour of `/pay` when the wagering detector or its log store is unavailable - a builder cannot decide whether `/pay` blocks when the transaction log or detector is down (Law 8) or proceeds unlogged (3.5.1's necessity claim).
- Third-party store versus indirect gating - 3.3 states access "cannot be limited or controlled, directly or indirectly, by a player owning or having access to out-of-game content, products, or services" and lists "a third-party queue or access platform" as not allowed, and 3.2 condition four says do not "hand operational control to a third party while still charging for access" VS 3.8 which mandates "Use a merchant of record (Tebex or equivalent)" - it matters because Tebex is an out-of-game third-party service that sits between payment and whitelisting - a builder cannot decide whether the whitelist grant may be driven by a Tebex webhook or must be issued by owner-controlled infrastructure with Tebex only as processor.
- Combat variance versus equality - 4.3 offers "either (a) all players are translated to current combat mechanics, or (b) accept the variance and say so" VS Law 3 "Identical price, identical access, identical capability. The only differences between two players are skill, time played, and reputation" and pillar 4 in 1.2 "Same price, same access, same features, same odds" - it matters because option (b) makes client version a capability difference on a server whose rank is decided by PvP alone - a builder cannot pick option (b) without the owner accepting a documented breach of Law 3.
- Bedrock feature parity versus Java-only cosmetics - 3.2 condition two requires "Everyone who pays gets access to all features and mods you enable" and 4.5 states "No setting may be reachable only through a specific client. That would violate Law 3" VS 4.4 which states "Bedrock players cannot install Java client mods" and "Geyser does not convert Java resource packs. Bedrock cosmetics need a separate Bedrock pack" - it matters because earned cosmetics (3.6, Section 11) delivered by a Java resource pack are unavailable to paying Bedrock customers - a builder cannot decide whether to fund a second Bedrock pack, restrict cosmetics to pack-free mechanisms, or accept a Mojang-condition breach.
- TPS as the requirement versus MSPT as the truth - 1.2 pillar 5 states "20 TPS is a product requirement, not an aspiration" VS Law 5 "MSPT and profiler output decide performance arguments. Not opinion, not 'it feels fine', not TPS alone. - Per-feature tick budget - 1.2 states "Every feature has a tick budget and is cut if it exceeds it" - no numeric budget, no measurement method, no unit is given in Sections 0 to 4 - what MSPT contribution, measured how and over what window, causes a feature to be cut?

- Combat-correlated payment window - 3.5.1 specifies a `/pay` "within roughly 60 seconds of a PvP death between those same two players" - "roughly" is not implementable, and the window's anchor (death event, respawn, or last damage tick) is undefined - is the window exactly 60 seconds, and is it measured from the death event timestamp?

## RISKS ON A 2 vCPU / 4 GB HOST

- Layer 3 text scanning runs a multi-phrase pattern match on every chat message, sign edit, anvil rename and book title. These are main-thread events, so a naive regex list on each one adds MSPT to the exact moment players are typing during combat.
- Layer 2 needs a self-join of the payment log against PvP deaths inside a 60-second window. On a small box this is a table-scan class query, and it must be scheduled off-peak and executed off the main thread or it will stall ticks.
- The detector requires a full season of retained transaction and chat data before any tuning. Sustained log growth on a 4 GB, single-volume VPS competes with world data for disk and with the JVM for page cache.
- Reciprocal-pair and house-pattern detection are multi-day windowed aggregations over the same tables, so the query cost grows with playerbase and history rather than staying flat.
- ViaVersion plus ViaBackwards plus ViaRewind add three protocol translation layers with per-packet CPU and per-connection memory. With rank decided by PvP, translation load peaks exactly when combat packet rates peak.
- Geyser and Floodgate add RakNet handling and Bedrock chunk and inventory translation. Running Geyser inside the Paper process on 2 vCPU puts that work in direct contention with the tick loop.
- Optional Apollo integration in 4.5 adds a further packet-level module for Lunar users, for a feature 4.5 itself labels "a nicety" and "a bonus", so its cost buys no required capability.
- The `-Xmx` headroom rule leaves roughly 3 GB or less of heap after container overhead, while Via, Geyser and a full economy plus logging stack all hold per-player state. Bedrock translation is the largest per-player memory consumer of the three.
- 1.4 concedes that "60 players fighting in one arena will not hold 20 TPS on this box", yet 1.2 states "20 TPS is a product requirement". Any arena or event feature must be capped in code, because the budget cannot satisfy both statements.
- A separate Bedrock resource pack, if produced for cosmetic parity, adds pack hosting bandwidth. Serving it from the game box would consume CPU and network on the same 2 vCPU allocation and should be hosted off-box.
- Section 3.9's repository-wide grep across `key`, `bet` and `crate` is a build-time cost only and carries no runtime tick risk, but it will need an allowlist to stay fast and reviewable as the repo grows.
