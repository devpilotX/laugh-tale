## CONTRADICTIONS

- Event MSPT allowance versus the global target - 12.6 requires "MSPT stays under 40 ms at the configured event cap with combat active" VS the project-wide sub-25 ms MSPT target that 11.5 and 6.6 depend on ("Under MSPT pressure - Automatically reduced, then disabled, by the watchdog") - a watchdog tuned to the global target will fire continuously throughout every war event, degrading the event it is supposed to protect - the owner must state whether the watchdog gets a raised threshold, a suppressed window, or an event-specific profile.
- "Champion" is two different things - 11.3 lists "Champion | Animated wings, kill effect" as an ordinary rank tier between Gladiator and Warlord, and separately lists "Season Champion | Crown plus the season-exclusive Champion set" VS 12.5 "There is exactly one Champion." - permission nodes, prefixes, broadcast text and the 9.6 advancement all need one unambiguous name, and players will read the rank as the title - the owner must rename one of the two.
- Disabling "all cosmetics" during events - 12.4 mitigation 3 says "Disable all cosmetics, holograms and non-essential scheduled tasks during the event window" VS 11.1 which rules resource-pack texture animation "Zero. Literally none." with the verdict "Keep. Unconditionally." - disabling pack-based capes and elytra buys no tick time at all and only removes earned visible status from paying players - the owner must confirm the event kill-switch covers particles only.
- Are event particles a default or a hard rule - 11.5 says "During war events - Particle cosmetics off by default for everyone" VS 12.3 which lists "Cosmetic particles off during events" as a "Hard mechanical requirement" - "off by default" implies a player-facing toggle that can be turned back on, a hard requirement implies no toggle exists - a builder cannot decide whether to ship the per-player override command.
- Finale field of 32 versus the measured event cap - 12.5 "Qualification: top 32 by RP at the season cutoff" VS 12.3 "Event caps: 20 to 24 now, 40 after the first migration, 60 at full scale" and 12.4 "Cap the event at the measured number" - 32 qualifiers exceeds the pre-migration cap of 20-24, so either the bracket must run in staggered heats or qualification must shrink - the owner must choose which, since it changes the Finale's shape and its marketing.
- Crash handling versus disconnect penalty - 12.3 "Disconnecting counts as losing a life" VS 12.6 "Inventories are perfectly restored on event exit, including after a crash mid-event" - a server crash or a host network blip disconnects every participant at once and would silently eliminate the whole field under the stated rule - the owner must approve a distinction between player-initiated quit and server-side loss, and there is no stated way to tell them apart reliably.
- Equal-access rationale versus the accepted fallback - 13.2 "This is the only answer that respects Law 3, because a voice system available to only some paying players is an unequal feature" VS 13.2 fallback "if it is not stable enough, fall back to option 1 for Bedrock players" and 13.5 which accepts a Bedrock player being "clearly told on the website that Discord is their route" - the accepted outcome is exactly the unequal feature the section forbids, since Discord is explicitly "not positional" in 13.1 - the owner must accept the inequality in writing or block launch on the bridge.
- Owner-only rollback versus the five-minute rollback SLA - 14.2 "Rollback, restore and log-purge are Owner-only. Admins get preview only." VS 14.10 "A full grief incident is rolled back inside five minutes using the documented workflow" - only one person on the server can complete the workflow, so the criterion is unmeetable whenever the owner is asleep or away, and 14.7 assumes "a small staff team can actually action" reports - the owner must either accept a delayed-response SLA or define an audited exception path.
- Violation log retention - 14.1 "Log every violation with the player, check, certainty, and timestamp, into the database, retained for the season" VS 14.4 "every punishment is logged with the issuing staff member's name, permanently and reviewably" - a permanent ban issued on anti-cheat evidence outlives the evidence that justified it, which breaks 14.7 appeals and 14.4's "evidence is required before any ban" - the owner must set a retention period for evidence attached to permanent punishments.
- Movement before rules acceptance - 14.5 "On first join, before the player can move, build or chat, they must accept the rules" VS 14.3 chat-flood defence "a first-join restriction such as requiring the player to move before chatting" - the two gates are mutually exclusive in order: one forbids movement until acceptance, the other requires movement before chat - a builder cannot sequence the join flow without a ruling.
- "Hard numbers" heading with no numbers - 11.5 is titled "The particle budget - hard numbers" VS its own rows "Maximum particles per player per tick | Capped and configurable", "Viewer radius | Limited", "Under MSPT pressure | Automatically reduced" - only the 4-tick interval and the one-trail-plus-one-wings limit are actual values - a builder must invent the cap, the radius and the degradation steps.
- Auto-punishment versus the no-auto-ban rule - 14.1 "Never auto-ban on a single flag. Auto-flag, auto-alert, and require a human decision plus recorded evidence for a permanent ban" VS 14.4 which sets "Cheating or unfair client mods - Ban 30 d plus full season RP reset to floor" as an automatic-looking first strike and 14.3 which prescribes "escalating automatic mutes" - the human-review requirement is written only for permanent bans, leaving a 30-day ban plus RP wipe arguably automatable - the owner must confirm that every cheating punishment, not only permanent ones, requires human sign-off.
- Cosmetics kept forever versus cheating penalty - 11.4 "Cosmetics are kept forever... A player who reaches Gladiator in Season 2 still has that animated cape in Season 20" VS 14.4 "Cheating or unfair client mods - Ban 30 d plus full season RP reset to floor" - a convicted cheater returns after 30 days still wearing the Legend or Mythic set earned while cheating, which undercuts 14.1's "a single undetected cheater corrupts the entire ladder" - the owner must decide whether cosmetics are revocable on a cheating conviction.

## RISKS ON A 2 vCPU / 4 GB HOST

- Particle cosmetics scale as players times viewers times particle count per 11.1, so a single crowded spawn or fight can multiply cost quadratically even inside the 4-tick interval; the viewer radius that is supposed to bound this has no stated value
- The 11.6 budget of under 2 ms MSPT for twenty maxed players consumes roughly 8 per cent of a 25 ms tick for pure decoration, before any combat, mob or chunk work
- 11.5 requires async particle dispatch "wherever the API permits", but Bukkit particle spawning is largely main-thread; the fallback path is a synchronous loop over online players, which is exactly what the section forbids
- A separate arena world per 12.4 means a second set of loaded chunks, entities and tickets resident alongside the survival world on 4 GB, which raises heap pressure and GC pause frequency during the single busiest hour of the week
- 12.3's cap of 20 to 24 concurrent players in sustained combat is the heaviest tick load the server will ever see: hit detection, projectiles, potion effects and 20-plus player entity trackers all on 2 cores
- Spectator-mode players are still tracked clients per 12.3, each consuming chunk streaming and packet bandwidth without contributing to the fight, so the effective player count during an event exceeds the participant cap
- Scripted arena regeneration between events per 12.3 is a large block-write and chunk-save burst; done on the main thread it stalls the server, and done on 2 cores it competes with everything else
- The Ender Dragon finale per 12.5 loads the End dimension on top of the survival and arena worlds and adds dragon pathfinding plus crystal entities and explosions while PvP is live, the worst combination in the document for a 2-core box
- Voice relay CPU and bandwidth scale as speakers times listeners per 13.3 at 20 to 32 kbps each; a 20-player proximity fight with several speakers is a continuous UDP packet-fanout load on the same two cores serving ticks
- The browser voice bridge per 13.2 adds a separate web service plus a TLS reverse proxy resident in the same 4 GB alongside Paper, the database and the Pelican panel
- Full block logging from day one per 14.2 writes a database row for every block change, adding sustained disk I/O and a MySQL working set to a 4 GB box shared with the server JVM
- Rolling back a full grief incident per 14.10 is a bulk block-edit and bulk database read in a five-minute window, which is a foreseeable MSPT spike with no stated rate limit
- Region plugin high-frequency flags for fluid and fire flow per 14.2 fire on every liquid and fire tick; the section itself flags the setting as performance-sensitive and requires measuring it
- Packet-level protection per 14.3 inspects every inbound packet, so its cost is per-packet and rises with player count, and it is mandatory before launch
- Anti-cheat running on netty threads per 14.1 does not consume tick time but still consumes CPU on the same 2 vCPU, so the "does not cost you tick time" property does not mean it is free on this box
- Chat flood defence must hold MSPT steady under 200 messages per second from one account per 14.10, meaning the rate limiter itself must be cheap and must reject before any formatting or broadcast work
- 12.4 mitigation 3 admits holograms and scheduled tasks must be disabled during events, which is evidence the steady-state background task load already has no headroom for an event
